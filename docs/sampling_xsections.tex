\author{J.J. G\'omez-Cadenas}

\begin{abstract}
Five fitted excitation functions, with correlated uncertainty ensembles,
describe the production of $^{11}$C, $^{13}$N and $^{15}$O by protons on the
nuclei of tissue.  This note explains, step by step, how those curves replace
the internal nuclear model of the transport code as the source of
positron-emitter production, while the transport itself is unchanged.  The
method is a per-step sampling: at every proton step, the fitted cross section
decides whether an emitter is born there.  The deliverable is the paired
comparison of the distal activity edge computed both ways,
$\Delta = R_{50}^{\mathrm{data}} - R_{50}^{\mathrm{G4}}$, with identical
proton histories, so the difference measures the production model alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Starting point: five fitted excitation functions}

The production of positron emitters by a therapeutic proton beam in tissue is
dominated by five reaction channels,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &^{12}\mathrm{C}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{15}\mathrm{O},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\\
  &^{14}\mathrm{N}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:channels}
\end{equation}
For each channel $c$ we possess a fitted excitation function
$\sigma_c(E)$ --- the production cross section as a function of proton kinetic
energy --- built from curated experimental data, together with an ensemble of
1000 correlated replica curves that represents its uncertainty.  The
construction of these curves (data selection, fitting, replica generation) is
documented in \file{docs/xsection\_fit.tex}.  Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows
the five nominal curves with their 16--84\% uncertainty bands and the
measurements they are built from; Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy} shows the complete
diagnostic anatomy of one fit.

with margin.

\section{What we want to compute}

The clinical observable of PET range verification is the distal edge of the
activity profile: the depth at which the emitter production falls to half of
its maximum, $R_{50}$.  The transport code (Geant4) predicts this edge using
its own internal nuclear model for the reactions in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:channels}.  The fitted curves of Fig.~\ref{fig:curves} are the
experimental knowledge of the same reactions.  The question this note
answers is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{by how much does the predicted distal edge move when the production
cross sections of the internal model are replaced by the fitted experimental
curves, everything else being identical?}
\end{quote}
The answer is one number per isotope (and one for the combined profile),
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_j = R_{50,j}^{\mathrm{data}} - R_{50,j}^{\mathrm{G4}},
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where ``data'' denotes production sampled from the fitted curves and ``G4''
denotes production from the internal model, both evaluated on the same proton
histories.

\section{Production probability of one proton step}

The transport code advances each proton (primary or secondary) in short
steps.  Consider one step of length $\ell$ (cm) through a material that
contains $n_t$ nuclei per cm$^3$ of target isotope $t$, taken by a proton of
kinetic energy $E$ and statistical weight $a$.  The expected number of
reactions through channel $c$ on this step is the number of target nuclei per
unit area seen by the proton, multiplied by the cross section:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{c} = a\, n_{t(c)}\,\ell\,\sigma_c(E),
  \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_c$ in cm$^2$ ($1~\mathrm{mb} = 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{cm}^2$).  For a
therapeutic beam $\mu_c$ is of order $10^{-6}$ per step: the expected count
is far below one, so $\mu_c$ is also, to excellent accuracy, the
\emph{probability} that one reaction happens on the step,
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{one reaction on the step}) = 1 - e^{-\mu_c} \simeq \mu_c .
  \label{eq:bernoulli}
\end{equation}

This gives the sampling rule.  At every proton step, for every channel open
at that energy, draw a uniform random number $u \in (0,1)$; if
$u < \mu_c$, one emitter of the channel's isotope is created at a point
chosen uniformly along the step.  The proton itself continues exactly as
before --- the sampled reaction changes nothing in its transport.  The
created emitter then follows its natural fate: the isotope decays with its
physical half-life, the positron slows down in the surrounding material and
annihilates, and the production and annihilation points are recorded.

Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu} over all steps of all protons gives the expected
production of channel $c$,
\begin{equation}
  N_c = \sum_{s} a_s\, n_{t(c),s}\,\ell_s\,\sigma_c(E_s),
  \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
and the sampled emitters are a Poisson realization of this expectation,
distributed in space exactly as production demands: where the proton fluence
is high, where the target nuclei are abundant, and where the cross section at
the local proton energy is large.

\section{Two tallies on the same protons}

The comparison runs both production models on \emph{identical} proton
histories.  During the same transport:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item the \textbf{data-driven tally} samples emitters with
        Eq.~\eqref{eq:bernoulli} from the fitted curves, as described above;
  \item the \textbf{native tally} counts the residual nuclei that the
        transport code's internal nuclear model produces --- the same five
        channels, recorded with production position and identity.
\end{enumerate}
Because both tallies watch the same protons step by step, the statistical
noise of the transport cancels in the comparison: $\Delta_j$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} measures the production model, not the luck of the run.

Two rules keep the calculation consistent.  First, the residual nuclei of
the five channels created by the internal model are counted but \emph{not}
turned into emitters --- production of these isotopes is claimed once, by the
fitted curves.  Second, the proton transport is left untouched by the
sampling.  This second rule deserves a closer look.

As the beam penetrates, protons are removed from it by nuclear collisions:
a proton that undergoes an inelastic collision with a nucleus scatters away
and leaves the beam, so the proton fluence decreases with depth --- roughly
one percent of the protons per centimetre of tissue.  The removal rate is
set by the \emph{total} inelastic cross section, the probability of a
nuclear collision with any outcome whatsoever, several hundred millibarns.
The five channels of Eq.~\eqref{eq:channels} are particular outcomes of
such collisions; together they amount to tens of millibarns, the fraction
\begin{equation}
  f(E)=\frac{\sum_c\sigma_c(E)}{\sigma_{\mathrm{inel}}(E)}
  \label{eq:fraction}
\end{equation}
of the total, about ten percent.

Replacing the five partial cross sections while the transport keeps the
total fixed introduces a first-order inconsistency: a channel whose fitted
cross section exceeds the internal model's should, strictly, remove slightly
more protons from the beam.  The size of the inconsistency is set by the
\emph{change} of the partial cross sections --- a few millibarns against
several hundred --- so the removal rate is altered by well below one
percent, and most of that cancels in the shape of the profile that
determines $R_{50}$.  The fraction $f(E)$ is the conservative ceiling of
the effect, and it is tracked as a diagnostic over the energies and
materials sampled by the treatment field.

\section{From production to the distal edge}

Binning the emitters of isotope $j$ in depth $z$ along the beam axis gives
the production profile $P_j(z)$; the sum over isotopes gives the combined
profile.  The distal edge is the depth beyond the profile maximum at which
the profile crosses half of that maximum.  With bin centres $z_k$ and
contents $P_k$, let $k^\ast$ be the last bin with
$P_{k^\ast} \ge \tfrac{1}{2}P_{\max}$; linear interpolation to the next bin
gives
\begin{equation}
  R_{50} = z_{k^\ast}
  + \left(\tfrac{1}{2}P_{\max} - P_{k^\ast}\right)
    \frac{z_{k^\ast+1}-z_{k^\ast}}{P_{k^\ast+1}-P_{k^\ast}} .
  \label{eq:r50}
\end{equation}
Absolute yields are reported per simulated run, per primary proton, and per
gray, using the proton count and the target dose recorded with the run.

Three features carry the physics.  The $^{15}$O yields of the two models
agree at the percent level, and their edges differ modestly.  For $^{11}$C
the native model produces no residuals below a proton energy of about
\SI{50}{MeV}, while the measurements establish production from the
\SI{17.9}{MeV} threshold; the missing near-threshold production sits at the
deepest part of the $^{11}$C profile, and the data-driven edge is therefore
several millimetres deeper.  For $^{13}$N the native model produces two
thirds of the measured yield and places its edge \SI{28}{mm} too shallow:
the measured production persists to the lowest energies, so its profile
reaches deepest of the three isotopes.  In the raw combined profile these
effects balance at this field, and the net displacement is consistent with
zero.
The scenario-weighted rows of Table~\ref{tab:delta} measure what that
balance is worth: applying the decay-and-acquisition factors of the named
timing scenarios reweights the isotopes, and the paired displacement moves
from $+0.19\pm0.58$ mm (in-room) to $+2.67\pm0.58$ mm (offline), where the
long delay suppresses $^{15}$O and the measured profile is carried by
$^{11}$C.

The native tally counts the residuals of all projectiles of the internal
model, so the comparison prices the complete replacement of the production
model; the projectile-resolved counters separate the proton-induced part
when required.

\section{The cross-section systematic of the distal edge}
\label{sec:systematic}

Replacing the internal production model by fitted curves does more than move
the edge to the measured place: it makes the edge's uncertainty a computable
number.  This section defines that number and reports it.

The fit of each channel provides, beyond the nominal curve, 1000 replica
curves --- complete alternative excitation functions, each consistent with
the world data, the quoted point errors, the campaign structure and the
documented normalizations (\file{docs/xsection\_fit.tex}).  A replica is a
coherent ``possible world'': one full set of five curves, correlated across
energy.  The uncertainty of any quantity computed from the curves is the
spread of that quantity over the replicas.

Propagation costs no new transport.  The proton exposure
$K_{t,k,m}$ --- how much beam crossed how much of target $t$ at energy bin
$k$ and depth bin $m$ --- does not depend on the production cross sections,
so one frozen transport serves every replica.  For replica $r$, the
production profile of isotope $j$ is the fold
\begin{equation}
  P_j^{(r)}(z_m) = \sum_{k}\sum_{c\to j} K_{t(c),k,m}\,
  \sigma_c^{(r)}(\bar E_{k}),
  \label{eq:replica-fold}
\end{equation}
its edge is $R_{50}^{(r)}$, and the paired displacement from the nominal
curve, $b^{(r)} = R_{50}^{(r)}-R_{50}^{(\mathrm{nom})}$, isolates the
cross-section variation from everything shared.  With $q_{16}$ and $q_{84}$
the percentiles of the $b^{(r)}$ distribution, the reported systematic is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{xs}}=\frac{q_{84}-q_{16}}{2}.
  \label{eq:uxs}
\end{equation}

One approximation enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:replica-fold}: the cross section is
evaluated at the exposure-weighted mean energy of each bin.  Its accuracy is
a measured property, not an assumption: coarsened tables, folded with all
1000 replicas and compared replica by replica, quantify the binning effect
directly.  On the frozen production grid of \SI{1.0}{MeV} the largest
paired edge change is \SI{0.002}{mm} --- two orders of magnitude below the
band --- while a \SI{2}{MeV} grid already fails at the $^{13}$N resonances
(\SI{0.134}{mm}).  The decision record lives in
\path{data/xsections/convergence}.

separate terms, accounted for downstream.

\section{One transported sample for every replica: the source bank}
\label{sec:bank}

The per-step sampling of Sec.~3 is exact for one curve set.  Propagating
the 1000 replicas through the positron and detector stages the same way
would cost 1000 proton transports and 1000 detector simulations.  Two facts
permit sharing one simulation among all curves: the proton transport does
not depend on the production cross sections (the sampler-on and sampler-off
runs are identical), and the fate of an emitter --- positron slowing,
annihilation, photons, detector response --- depends only on its isotope
and position, never on the curve that priced its birth.

\subsection{Definition}

During the one proton transport, segment--channel candidates $(s,c)$ are
kept with probability
\begin{equation}
  q_{s,c} = \min\!\bigl(1,\;
  \alpha\, n_{t(c),s}\,\ell_s\,\sigma^{\mathrm{env}}_{c}(E_s)\bigr),
  \label{eq:bank-q}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{\mathrm{env}}_{c}(E)$ is a fixed curve chosen at or above
every replica of channel $c$, and the constant $\alpha$ sets the bank size.
Each kept entry stores a permanent identifier, the channel (hence target
and isotope), the position, the proton energy $E_s$, the material factor
$n_{t(c),s}\ell_s$, and $q_{s,c}$ itself.

\subsection{Unbiasedness}

For any curve $\sigma_c$ and any spatial bin $m$, the bank estimator of the
production is the sum over kept entries of the entry weight
\begin{equation}
  w_{s,c} = \frac{n_{t(c),s}\,\ell_s\,\sigma_c(E_s)}{q_{s,c}}.
  \label{eq:bank-weight}
\end{equation}
Its expectation over the keep/discard draws is exact,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{E}\Bigl[\sum_{(s,c)\ \mathrm{kept},\ s\in m} w_{s,c}\Bigr]
  = \sum_{(s,c),\ s\in m} n_{t(c),s}\,\ell_s\,\sigma_c(E_s)
  = P_{c,m},
  \label{eq:bank-unbiased}
\end{equation}
because each candidate enters with probability $q_{s,c}$ and contributes
$1/q_{s,c}$ when it does.  The sampling curve cancels identically: it never
appears in a physics result, only in the noise.  The one requirement is
$q_{s,c}>0$ wherever any replica produces, which the envelope guarantees.

\subsection{Weight control}

For replica $r$ the weight is proportional to
$\sigma^{(r)}_c(E)/\sigma^{\mathrm{env}}_c(E)\le 1$: bounded above by
construction, so no replica's estimate is carried by a few inflated
entries.  The efficiency of the bank for a given replica and channel is
measured by the effective sample size
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ESS} = \frac{\bigl(\sum w\bigr)^2}{\sum w^2},
  \label{eq:ess}
\end{equation}
the number of equally weighted entries the weighted set is worth.  The
acceptance criterion of the implementation is an ESS floor per channel and
per replica.

\subsection{Use}

Every bank entry is transported once: the residual ion created at rest at
its position, the positron to its annihilation point, and downstream the
two photons through phantom and scanner, every recorded signal carrying the
entry's identifier.  Any observable of replica $r$ --- production profile,
isotope budget, activity profile, detected image --- is then the \emph{same}
recorded sample summed with the weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bank-weight}
evaluated at $\sigma^{(r)}$:
\begin{equation}
  P^{(r)}_{j}(z_m)=\sum_{\mathrm{kept}\ (s,c),\, c\to j,\, s\in m}
  w^{(r)}_{s,c},
  \qquad
  A^{(r)}_{j}(z_m)=f_j\,P^{(r)}_{j}(z_m),
  \label{eq:bank-observables}
\end{equation}
with $f_j$ the per-isotope decay-and-acquisition factor of the named timing
scenario.  Evaluating a replica costs one interpolation of its curves at
the stored energies and one division per entry; the thousand-replica
ensemble costs arithmetic, not simulation.

\subsection{Acceptance}

Two checks certify a bank before use.  The weighted nominal must reproduce
the independently sampled nominal source of Sec.~3 --- the naive procedure
as referee --- in yields, profiles and edges within their statistics; and
the ESS floor must hold for every channel and every replica.  The measured
values enter here when the implementation runs.

\section{Conclusions}

The comparison separates cleanly what the transport code does well from
what it cannot do.

\emph{The transport is sound and is kept.}  The application used here
reproduces the established Stage-A calculation with the same beam, phantom
and physics settings: the target dose per proton agrees to $0.02\%$, the
central-axis dose profile to a fraction of a percent across the field, and
the distal dose edge to \SI{0.024}{mm}.  The positron ranges of the sampled
emitters match the per-isotope references.  Geant4 is the transport engine
of this calculation, and every number in this note relies on it.

\emph{The native production model is disqualified for activity
calculations.}  Its per-isotope predictions are wrong at the scale that
matters for range verification: the $^{11}$C edge by $+5.0\pm0.9$~mm
(production absent below $\sim$\SI{50}{MeV} against a measured threshold of
\SI{17.9}{MeV}), the $^{13}$N yield by a factor 1.5 and its edge by
$+28\pm4$~mm, the $^{15}$O edge by $-1.8\pm0.5$~mm, the total yield by
10\%, and the offline-weighted activity by 21\%.  A technique whose
specification is millimetre accuracy on an activity edge cannot be built on
a production model with millimetre-to-centimetre edge errors per isotope.

\emph{The combined-profile agreement is compensation, and the acquisition
timing exposes it.}  The raw combined displacement, $-0.03\pm0.62$~mm,
comes from opposite-signed per-isotope errors cancelling at this particular
field and isotope mix.  Reweighting the same profiles by the acquisition
scenarios moves the displacement to $+0.11\pm0.67$~mm (fast),
$+0.19\pm0.58$~mm (in-room) and $+2.67\pm0.58$~mm (offline): the error of
the native model in the measured edge depends on when one images.  A model
wrong isotope by isotope is right in combination only by accident, and the
accident does not survive a change of timing.

The calculation therefore takes its production from the fitted measurements
and its transport from Geant4.  The same sampling machinery extends
directly to the uncertainty band: each of the 1000 replica curves defines
its own $\sigma_c(E)$, and the spread of the resulting edges gives the
cross-section uncertainty of $R_{50}$ --- at this field a band of about
$\pm0.2$~mm on the production edge, an order of magnitude below the model
displacement it replaces.  That propagation, and its efficient
implementation, are specified in \file{docs/xsections\_plan.tex} and
\file{docs/shared\_plan.tex}.
