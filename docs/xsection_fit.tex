\title{Selection, curation, and uncertainty modelling of PET-production
cross sections}
\author{J.J. G\'omez-Cadenas}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Experimental excitation functions are used to construct cross-section
ensembles for five positron-emitter production channels relevant to
proton-therapy PET.  The input is a pinned EXFOR snapshot subjected to explicit
dataset- and point-level selection.  Positive, threshold-constrained spline
fits retain the separation between experimental campaigns and account for
reported point uncertainties and observed between-campaign dispersion.
JENDL-4.0/HE and LANL ENDF/B-VII.1 provide external comparisons and do not
enter the fit.  The nominal curve and its replicas are folded with
target-resolved proton track length from a frozen Geant4 transport and
propagated to the production and reconstructed distal activity edges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reaction scope and role in the uncertainty calculation}

The selected residual-production channels are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &^{12}\mathrm{C}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{15}\mathrm{O},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\\
  &^{14}\mathrm{N}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The fitted interval is \SIrange{5}{150}{MeV}.  This interval contains the
low-energy production that controls the distal activity fall-off and the
incident-energy range of the treatment field.  The extension beyond the
highest treatment layer constrains the upper fit boundary with measured data.
The fit uses experimental cross sections.  Geant4 subsequently supplies the
proton trajectories, energy loss, fluence and total inelastic attenuation.
Production through the five selected channels is calculated from the fitted
curves; the corresponding Geant4 residual-production cross sections and final-
state branching do not enter either the nominal result or its replicas.

\section{Experimental data and evaluated comparisons}

\subsection{EXFOR snapshot and normalized representation}

The experimental input is the EXFOR Plot File snapshot at commit
\texttt{57052199247e}, dated 29 June 2026; the complete commit identifier is
stored in the source manifest.  The selected snapshot contains 85
excitation-function series for the five channels.  Each CX4 series is converted
one-to-one into the common schema under \path{data/xsections/normalized}; the
conversion changes eV to MeV and b to mb and preserves the EXFOR accession,
reaction field, source reference, central values, and reported uncertainties.

Experimental campaigns remain separate.  The EXFOR accession prefix defines
the campaign identifier, so subentries obtained from the same irradiation
share a campaign-level contribution in the covariance model.

\subsection{Evaluated libraries}

JENDL-4.0/HE and the LANL proton evaluations distributed with ENDF/B-VII.1 are
converted to the same energy and cross-section units.  They are compared with
the resulting experimental fit.  Their values are not treated as
measurements and do not contribute to the nominal curve or its uncertainty
band.

\section{Data curation}

\subsection{Dataset-level selection}

Dataset-level curation is based on the normalized EXFOR metadata.  A series is
accepted when
\begin{enumerate}
  \item its projectile, target isotope, and residual isotope identify one of
        the five selected channels;
  \item the EXFOR reaction field identifies an absolute production cross
        section, with no relative-yield, abundance, thick-target-yield,
        monitor-ratio, or cumulative modifier;
  \item the original units are eV and b and therefore have a direct conversion
        to MeV and mb; and
  \item the normalized quantity is identified as a cross section.
\end{enumerate}
The accepted EXFOR reaction suffixes are \texttt{,,SIG},
\texttt{,,SIG,,,EXP}, and \texttt{,IND,SIG,,,EXP}.  A dataset that fails one
of these tests is assigned either \emph{excluded} or \emph{pending
verification}.  Numerical disagreement between experiments is not, by itself,
an exclusion criterion.

Six series are pending verification.  The EXFOR reaction fields for B0077.002
and B0077.003 contain the SF8 modifier \texttt{A}, which denotes a cross
section multiplied by isotopic abundance.\footnote{IAEA,
\href{https://nds.iaea.org/nrdc/nrdc_doc/iaea-nds-0208-200601.pdf}{LEXFOR,
General Quantity Modifiers and Products}.}  The stored values are consequently
not direct absolute cross sections.  These two series remain outside the fit
until the abundance factor and normalization are recovered from the original
entry and publication.

B0095.002 contains two measurements at 20.7 and \SI{21.1}{MeV}.  Its EXFOR
incident-energy field is \SI{1}{MeV}, but the present curation has not
established whether this field denotes calibration uncertainty, resolution,
or an energy interval.  The series is therefore pending and is omitted from
the nominal fit.  A named sensitivity fit includes B0095.002 together with all
nominally accepted carbon data.

Masuda E2568.002, E2568.003, and E2568.004 are relative excitation functions
normalized simultaneously to
$^{16}$O(p,x)$^{15}$O = \SI{76.8}{mb} at \SI{35}{MeV}.  Their shared external
normalization is not represented by the present covariance model.  These
series are retained in the figures at their published scale as shape
comparisons and are pending for the absolute fit.

The metadata criteria provide a reproducible initial selection.  Verification
against an original publication is recorded as a separate curation action when
the EXFOR quantity or normalization is ambiguous; the procedure does not infer
an undocumented uncertainty or normalization component.

\subsection{Point-level selection}

Within an accepted series, a point contributes to the weighted fit when
\begin{equation}
  5\leq E\leq150~\mathrm{MeV},\qquad
  E>E_{\mathrm{th}},\qquad
  \sigma>0,
\end{equation}
and EXFOR reports a positive cross-section uncertainty.  The adopted thresholds
are 17.9, 14.29, 25.17, 8.9, and \SI{5.55}{MeV}, respectively, in the channel
order given above.  For the two corrected oxygen channels, Table~\ref{tab:thresholds}
gives the lowest production route and the exact laboratory-frame threshold
calculated from AME2020 atomic masses.  The calculation uses
\begin{equation}
 E_{\mathrm{th,lab}}=
 \frac{M_f^2-(M_p+M_T)^2}{2M_T}c^2,
\end{equation}
where $M_f$ is the sum of the final-state rest masses.  The rounded values in
the fit configuration are checked against the generated calculation.

\section{Cross-section model}

\subsection{Threshold-constrained smooth curve}

For $E>E_{\mathrm{th}}$, the cross section is represented as
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(E)=(E-E_{\mathrm{th}})^{1/2}\exp[g(E)],
  \label{eq:curve}
\end{equation}
where $g(E)$ is a cubic B-spline.  Equation~\eqref{eq:curve} imposes a zero
cross section at and below threshold and a positive cross section above it.  A
squared second-difference penalty controls the spline curvature.

The fit is performed in the transformed variable
\begin{equation}
  g_i=\log\sigma_i-\frac{1}{2}\log(E_i-E_{\mathrm{th}}).
\end{equation}
A reported fractional one-standard-deviation cross-section error $r_i$ is
mapped to the logarithmic error
\begin{equation}
  u_i=\sqrt{\log(1+r_i^2)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Campaign covariance and smoothing selection}

For experimental points $i$ and $j$, the covariance model is
\begin{equation}
  C_{ij}=u_i^2\delta_{ij}+s_{c(i)}^2\, I[c(i)=c(j)],
  \label{eq:covariance}
\end{equation}
where $c(i)$ denotes the campaign.  The second term represents a coherent
displacement of a campaign rather than independent fluctuations of all its
points.  A campaign whose source documents its normalization uncertainty
uses that documented value as its offset scale $s_c$, anchoring the level of
the fitted curve at the precision its authors established; the documented
components and citations accompany the values in
\path{config/xsection_fit.toml} (Akagi 2013 from the EXFOR error analysis;
Rodr\'iguez-Gonz\'alez 2022 and 2023 from the published budgets).  The
remaining campaigns share one fitted spread $s_c$.  A level set by one equal
vote per campaign would hand the plateau of a channel to the region where
campaigns are most numerous and least consistent; the anchors give the level
to the campaigns that measured it.

Two channel-level decisions accompany the anchors.  In
$^{16}$O(p,x)$^{11}$C the documented Akagi normalization is contradicted by
the monitor-validated 2023 campaign and by the independent campaigns, which
sit 25--70\% above its level; its anchor is withdrawn in that channel (the
configuration records the reason) and its points remain in the fit under the
fitted spread.  $^{16}$O(p,x)$^{13}$N is fitted in two segments joined at
\SI{22}{MeV}: the single-segment model transmits the level of the
resonance-region campaigns --- numerous and mutually discrepant --- into the
plateau through the shared offsets, placing the curve 25\% above four
mutually consistent plateau campaigns.  Each segment carries its own
campaign-offset model, and the two curves are blended geometrically over
\SI{2}{MeV}.

The curvature penalty and the shared $s_c$ are selected from fixed grids by
leave-one-campaign-out prediction, per segment where a channel is split.  Each complete campaign is predicted by a
fit to the remaining campaigns, and the selection score is the Gaussian
predictive loss including its variance term.  The fractional campaign spread
reported in the results is $\exp(s_c)-1$.

Spline knots are dense below \SI{100}{MeV}, where the threshold structure and
excitation-function maxima occur, and coarser above \SI{100}{MeV}.  The latter
region is constrained by accepted measurements through \SI{150}{MeV}; this
placement suppresses free-boundary oscillation while retaining the measured
high-energy trend.

\section{Replica construction and cross-section uncertainty}

One thousand replicas are generated with a recorded random seed.  For each
replica, every fitted point receives a draw from its reported logarithmic
cross-section error and every campaign receives one common draw with its
offset scale: the documented normalization for anchored campaigns, the
fitted $s_c$ for the others.  The curvature penalty is reselected from the
fixed grid, and the varied data are refitted.

No CX4 energy field has yet been verified as an uncertainty rather than an
energy interval, so the energy-displacement variation remains inactive;
adding a verified value activates it without changing the procedure.

At each energy, the replica median defines the nominal cross section.  The
16th and 84th percentiles define the primary uncertainty interval.  Its
relative half-width is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{rel}}(E)=
  \frac{\sigma_{84}(E)-\sigma_{16}(E)}{2\sigma_{50}(E)}.
\end{equation}

\section{Replica-distance diagnostic}

For plotting purposes, replica $r$ is assigned the distance
\begin{equation}
  D_r=\frac{1}{N}[g_r-g_{50}]^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}[g_r-g_{50}],
  \label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the $N$ fitted experimental energies.  The histogram in each
figure contains all replicas, and the nine displayed curves are selected at
fixed quantiles of $D$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:distance} is a diagnostic of the replica ensemble.  Its
magnitude depends on the channel covariance, the number and placement of the
data, and the selected curvature penalty.  It is not used to rank channel
uncertainties and does not enter the propagated distal-edge uncertainty.
The curvature penalty is reselected from a discrete grid for every replica.
Consequently, replicas assigned to different penalty values can form separate
modes in the $D$ histogram.  The counts and distance range for each selected
penalty are recorded in
\path{\generateddir/distance_by_smoothing.csv}; the distance percentile is
used only to choose the nine displayed examples.

\section{Propagation to production profiles and the distal activity edge}

\subsection{Frozen proton exposure}

The propagation begins with one Geant4 transport of primary and secondary
protons through the phantom.  For target isotope $t$, proton-energy bin $k$
and depth bin $m$, the scorer accumulates
\begin{equation}
  K_{t,k,m}=\sum_{s\in(t,k,m)}a_s n_{t,s}\ell_s,
  \label{eq:exposure}
\end{equation}
where $a_s$, $n_{t,s}$ and $\ell_s$ are the statistical weight, target number
density and length of proton step $s$.  The units of $K$ are cm$^{-2}$.  The
same weighted sums define the mean energy and depth stored for each occupied
bin.  The resulting CSV contains the target isotope, the energy and depth
limits, the two exposure-weighted means, and $K_{t,k,m}$.

The companion \path{exposure_meta.json} records the exposure-file digest,
transport-run identifier, number of primary protons, target-box dose, Geant4
version, physics list, random seed, software revision, coordinate definition,
and authoritative energy and depth edges.  The loader checks every bin against
these grids and verifies
\begin{equation}
  N_{p/\mathrm{Gy}}=\frac{N_p}{D_{\mathrm{sim,target}}}.
  \label{eq:normalization-check}
\end{equation}
Overlapping energy bins, overlapping depth bins, duplicate cells, inconsistent
normalization, and a file-digest mismatch stop the calculation.

Geant4 determines charged-particle transport, continuous energy loss, nuclear
attenuation and dose.  The same exposure table is used for the nominal curve
and all 1000 replicas.  Alternative BIC, INCL++ and Bertini calculations vary
the exposure table and constitute a separate transport envelope.

\subsection{Direct folding of the cross-section ensemble}

For channel $c$ with target $t(c)$, the expected production in one occupied
exposure cell is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta P_{c,k,m}^{(r)}=
  K_{t(c),k,m}\,
  \sigma_c^{(r)}(\bar E_{t(c),k,m})\,10^{-27},
  \label{eq:binned-fold}
\end{equation}
where the cross section is in millibarns and $10^{-27}$ converts millibarns to
cm$^2$.  Linear interpolation is used inside the fitted grid.  Production is
zero at and below the reaction threshold, and queries above the fitted range
are rejected.  The fitted grid is required to bracket the physical threshold.

Equation~\eqref{eq:binned-fold} is the binned implementation of the per-step
track-length estimator
\begin{equation}
  \Delta N_{s,c}^{(r)}=
  a_s n_{t(c),s}\ell_s\sigma_c^{(r)}(E_s).
  \label{eq:fold-step}
\end{equation}
The nominal calculation uses the stored pointwise replica median
$\sigma_c^{(50)}(E)$.  A replica calculation uses all five curves carrying the
same replica identifier.  Consequently, the variation is correlated across
energy, depth, and all uses of a channel curve.

Channel contributions producing the same residual are summed to obtain
\begin{equation}
  P_{j,m}^{(r)}=
  \sum_{c\rightarrow j}\sum_k\Delta P_{c,k,m}^{(r)}.
  \label{eq:isotope-profile}
\end{equation}
The three isotope profiles are $^{11}$C, $^{15}$O and $^{13}$N.
The selected-channel source is constructed from Eq.~\eqref{eq:binned-fold}.
Native Geant4 residuals provide a separate counter for neutron-induced
production, $^{10}$C, $^{14}$O and other routes outside the selected set.  The
counter is reported for raw production and after the named beam-off timing; it
serves as a diagnostic of the five-channel approximation.

The exposure is held fixed across cross-section replicas.  This retains the
nominal Geant4 total-inelastic attenuation while varying the selected partial
production cross sections.  The summed selected cross section divided by the
Geant4 total inelastic cross section is recorded as the scale of the associated
first-order attenuation correction.

\path{all_production} is their unweighted sum.  The named in-room profile is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{inroom},m}^{(r)}=
  \sum_j F_j P_{j,m}^{(r)},
  \label{eq:inroom-profile}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  F_j=
  \frac{1-\exp(-\lambda_j t_{\mathrm{irr}})}
       {\lambda_j t_{\mathrm{irr}}}
  \exp(-\lambda_j t_{\mathrm{del}})
  [1-\exp(-\lambda_j t_{\mathrm{meas}})].
  \label{eq:handoff-factor}
\end{equation}
The times are resolved from the named \path{inroom} entry in
\path{config/handoff_scenarios.toml}; the folding calculation and the handoff
budget use the same resolver and configuration digest.

Every profile and integral is reported for the simulated run, per primary
proton and per Gy.  The output consists of nominal channel contributions,
nominal isotope and aggregate profiles, pointwise replica bands, optional
compressed per-replica profiles, the complete production summary, an
uncertainty summary, and \path{folding_meta.json}.  The metadata retain the
cross-section, exposure, timing and software digests.

\subsection{Production-edge estimator and replica uncertainty}

For each profile, $R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}$ is the distal crossing of half the
profile maximum.  The algorithm selects the last depth bin at or above the
half maximum and linearly interpolates to the following bin.  Replica $r$ is
paired with the nominal result through
\begin{equation}
  b_{\mathrm{prod}}^{(r)}=
  R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}^{(r)}-
  R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}^{(50)}.
  \label{eq:production-shift}
\end{equation}
The yield diagnostic is the integral ratio
\begin{equation}
  \rho_Y^{(r)}=
  \frac{\sum_m P_m^{(r)}}{\sum_m P_m^{(50)}}.
  \label{eq:yield-ratio}
\end{equation}
The 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of $b_{\mathrm{prod}}$ define its central
shift and interval.  The reported symmetric summary is
$(q_{84}-q_{16})/2$.  The same percentile construction is applied to the
integral yield.  All replicas enter these distributions with equal weight;
the curve-distance diagnostic $D_r$ is used only to select representative
curves for the cross-section figures.

The reconstructed displacement is evaluated downstream with the same
definition,
\begin{equation}
  b_{\mathrm{reco}}^{(r)}=
  R_{50,\mathrm{reco}}^{(r)}-
  R_{50,\mathrm{reco}}^{(50)}.
\end{equation}
A common proposal sample drawn from the scored proton segments supplies
three-dimensional production sites for positron transport.  Nominal and
replica sources share proposal sites and random-number streams, preserving the
pairing through positron transport, beam-off acquisition, detector simulation,
reconstruction and the final edge estimator.

\subsection{Energy-grid convergence}

The initial transport uses a deliberately fine proton-energy grid.  Candidate
coarser tables are derived offline from the same exposure realization.  If
$G$ is a set of fine cells forming one coarse cell, the exact recombination is
\begin{equation}
  K_G=\sum_{g\in G}K_g,
  \qquad
  \bar E_G=\frac{\sum_{g\in G}K_g\bar E_g}{K_G}.
  \label{eq:coarsening}
\end{equation}
The approximation under test is therefore the use of
$\sigma(\bar E_G)$ for a cross section that can be nonlinear inside $G$.

The nominal curve and every replica are folded on the fine and candidate
grids.  For every isotope and aggregate profile, the program records the
largest paired change in yield and $R_{50}$ across the nominal result and all
replicas.  A candidate passes when the yield and $R_{50}$ changes are each
below 10\% of the corresponding fine-grid replica half-width, the absolute
$R_{50}$ change is below SI{0.1}{mm}, and the reported half-widths satisfy the
same stability requirement.  The accepted grid is fixed before the
high-statistics transport.

\subsection{Quantitative diagnostic figures}

The tracked program
\path{analysis_transport/xsections/make_folding_plots.py} reads the folding
products and \path{exposure_convergence.csv}.  It validates the required
columns, profile labels, depth ordering, quantile ordering, positive nominal

\subsubsection{Analytic reference calculation}

The figures below are produced before the Geant4 exposure run as an executable
reference for the complete propagation.  The reference uses the fitted
cross-section median and all 1000 fitted replicas with a deterministic analytic
proton-exposure field.  It tests the same interpolation, channel aggregation,
timing, normalization, edge extraction, uncertainty summaries, offline
coarsening and plotting code used for the physical result.

The reference depth grid covers \SIrange{0}{122}{mm} in \SI{2}{mm} bins and
the fine energy grid covers \SIrange{5}{120}{MeV} in \SI{0.5}{MeV} bins.  For
depth $z$ below the reference range $R=\SI{112}{mm}$, the mean energy is
\begin{equation}
 \bar E(z)=5+(112-5)
 \left[\frac{R-z}{R}\right]^{0.56}\ \mathrm{MeV}.
 \label{eq:reference-energy}
\end{equation}
The exposure is distributed around this mean with a Gaussian width of
\SI{3}{MeV}.  Its longitudinal amplitude is proportional to
$1-0.12z/R$.  The relative target-exposure coefficients are 0.12, 0.03 and
0.55 for $^{12}$C, $^{14}$N and $^{16}$O.  Beyond $R$, the reference exposure
is placed in the lowest energy bin, below all configured production
thresholds, to supply explicit zero-production bins for distal-edge
interpolation.

The absolute exposure scale and the assigned proton count and dose are fixed
reference constants; the reference quantities are the profile shapes,
replica-to-nominal ratios, paired edge shifts and convergence ratios.  Candidate energy widths of 0.5, 1, 2
and \SI{5}{MeV} are obtained by exact recombination of the fine table.  The
complete definition, fit and timing digests, numerical profiles, replica
summaries and convergence table are stored under
\path{docs/generated/xsection_folding/reference}.  They are regenerated by
\path{analysis_transport/xsections/make_folding_reference.py}.

\section{The effect of transport}
\label{sec:transport-term}

The folded prediction rests on two inputs: the fitted cross sections and
the proton exposure map that Geant4 transports.  The hadronic model shapes
that map through two channels --- the secondary protons its final-state
model adds to the beam, and the nonelastic attenuation of the primary
fluence.  The stopping power that places the edge is electromagnetic and
common to all models.

Both channels are varied directly.  The exposure run of the standard field
is repeated with the Bertini and the Li\`ege intranuclear cascades in
place of the binary-cascade reference, and each map is folded with the
same nominal curves; the three models share Geant4's proton inelastic
cross-section dataset, so this swap varies the secondary-proton term at
fixed attenuation.  The attenuation itself is varied by $\pm3\%$ --- the
scale of the differences between modern inelastic evaluations ---
multiplying the folded profiles by $e^{\mp\delta\Sigma z}$, with
$\Sigma = 0.010$~cm$^{-1}$ the nonelastic macroscopic cross section of the
brain phantom.  A fold of the tenfold-larger reference exposure bounds the
statistical scale of the comparison below \SI{0.01}{mm}.

The result is the $u_{\mathrm{transport}}$ column of
Table~\ref{tab:consequences}: $\pm0.09$~mm on the combined production edge
and $\pm0.10$~mm under the reference scan, dominated by the Li\`ege-cascade
displacement, with integral yields stable to $0.5\%$ across the models.
On the combined edges the transport term and the cross-section band are of
the same order, and their quadrature sum stays below $\pm0.2$~mm.  The
measurement is documented in \path{docs/sampling_xsections.tex} and
regenerates with \path{analysis_transport/xsections/transport_envelope.py}.

\section{Conclusions}

The curation retains 79 absolute EXFOR series for the five selected production
channels and assigns six series to pending verification.  The fitted ensembles
preserve reported point errors, coherent campaign variation, and uncertainty
in the smooth representation.  Folding each complete curve with a common
Geant4 proton track-length field transfers this uncertainty to isotope
budgets and spatial production profiles.  The between-campaign dispersion is
channel dependent and is largest for $^{16}$O(p,x)$^{13}$N in the present
selection.  Propagated to the standard field, the curves contribute
$\pm2$--$3\%$ to the yields per gray and $\pm0.13$~mm to the combined
production edge, and the transport of the exposure map contributes a term
of the same order beside it.
