\begin{abstract}
Experimental EXFOR data define the nominal excitation functions and 1000
correlated replicas for five positron-emitter production channels.  This note
specifies how these curves are combined with one frozen Geant4 proton
transport.  Target-resolved proton track length is folded directly with each
external cross section to calculate emitter production.  Geant4 determines
the charged-particle transport, energy loss, total inelastic attenuation and
dose; the fitted experimental curves determine production through the selected
channels.  The same transport realization is used for the nominal curve and
all replicas.  Complete transports with BIC, INCL++ and Bertini form a separate
hadronic-transport envelope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objective and frozen scope}

The first implementation covers
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &^{12}\mathrm{C}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{15}\mathrm{O},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{11}\mathrm{C},\\
  &^{14}\mathrm{N}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N},\qquad
   ^{16}\mathrm{O}(p,x)^{13}\mathrm{N}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:channels}
\end{equation}
The nominal cross sections and their replica ensembles are documented in
\file{docs/xsection\_fit.tex}.  JENDL-4.0/HE and LANL ENDF/B-VII.1 remain
comparisons with the fitted experimental curves.  The distal-edge calculation
uses the EXFOR fit and replicas.

The direct fold is the selected-channel production estimator.  The nominal
calculation and its replicas use the external curves.  The forced-final-state
Geant4 curves are residual-model diagnostics.  The BIC, INCL++ and Bertini
envelope varies the proton exposure and spatial proposal sample while retaining
the external production curves.

Two quantities are reported for replica $r$:
\begin{align}
  b_{\mathrm{prod}}^{(r)} & =
  R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}^{(r)}-R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}^{(\mathrm{nom})},
  \label{eq:bprod}\\
  b_{\mathrm{reco}}^{(r)} & =
  R_{50,\mathrm{reco}}^{(r)}-R_{50,\mathrm{reco}}^{(\mathrm{nom})}.
  \label{eq:breco}
\end{align}
Here ``nom'' denotes the pointwise median of the replica ensemble, stored as
the nominal curve.
The first displacement is available from the production profile in this
repository.  The second follows the common acquisition, detector simulation,
reconstruction and edge estimator.

The calculation separates two sources of variation:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item the \emph{experimental cross-section uncertainty}, obtained by folding
        every replica with the same frozen proton transport; and
  \item the \emph{hadronic-transport envelope}, obtained by repeating the
        transport with BIC, INCL++ and Bertini and folding the same external
        cross-section curves with each resulting track-length field.
\end{enumerate}
The two results are reported separately.  The first measures uncertainty in
the selected production cross sections at fixed transport.  The second
measures the dependence of proton and secondary-proton fluence on the Geant4
cascade model.

\section{Calculation architecture}

The calculation is organized as
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.91\textwidth}
Geant4 proton transport
$\longrightarrow$ target-resolved proton step history
$\longrightarrow$ track-length folding with the EXFOR curves
$\longrightarrow$ isotope production budgets and spatial profiles
$\longrightarrow$ positron transport and annihilation source
$\longrightarrow$ beam-off handoff and reconstructed $R_{50}$.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\subsection{Independent Geant4 application}

The calculation will be implemented as a new standalone application under
\file{stageA\_xsection\_ensemble/}.  It has its own CMake target, detector
construction, primary generator, action classes, macros, run scripts,
documentation and tests.  It neither links source files from nor modifies
\file{stageA\_transport}.  The two applications may read the same versioned
SOBP-layer and material inputs, and their agreement in geometry, dose and
depth dose is an explicit validation rather than an implementation dependency.

The new application has two modes, implemented as separate executables or as
two explicit run modes with disjoint output contracts:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item the \emph{proton-exposure mode} transports primary and secondary
        protons once, writes the target-resolved exposure and a spatial
        proposal bank, and records dose and native-production diagnostics;
  \item the \emph{variant-source mode} reads the frozen proposal bank and one
        nominal or replica cross-section set, samples the externally defined
        production source, transports the emitted positron, and writes the
        production and annihilation information required by the detector
        simulation.
\end{enumerate}
The first mode is common to the nominal curve and all replicas.  The second is
run for the nominal case and for the selected replica subset propagated through
the full detector.  It can therefore be repeated without repeating proton
transport.

The physical calculation begins with one proton-exposure run.  It records
steps from primary and secondary protons in the phantom and accumulates the
target-resolved proton exposure needed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:step-yield}.  The
required step information is
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item event and proton-track identifiers;
  \item pre- and post-step position and step length;
  \item pre- and post-step proton kinetic energy;
  \item material or the number densities of $^{12}$C, $^{14}$N and $^{16}$O;
  \item proton statistical weight.
\end{itemize}
A representative step energy is obtained from the pre- and post-step energies.
The adopted midpoint or path-averaged rule will be tested by subdividing the
steps.

Two tracked products from the exposure mode serve different stages of the calculation.
First, a binned target-resolved proton exposure table stores
\begin{equation}
 K_{t,k,m}=\sum_{s\in(t,k,m)}a_s n_{t,s}\ell_s,
 \label{eq:exposure-table}
\end{equation}
for target isotope $t$, proton-energy bin $k$ and spatial bin $m$.  It is a
multidimensional histogram rather than a fitted function.  Its units are
inverse area, and multiplication by a cross section gives the expected number
of reactions.  This compact table is sufficient for isotope budgets,
production profiles and $R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}$.  Second, a fixed proposal
sample of step segments retains their positions, energies, targets, path
lengths, statistical weights and sampling probabilities.  It supplies
production sites to the variant-source mode without storing the complete raw
step history separately for every replica.

Development and validation of the folding calculation precede the dedicated
Geant4 run.  Synthetic exposure tables with analytic yields establish the data
schema, units, interpolation, channel aggregation and edge calculation.  The
subsequent Geant4 scorer writes the physical exposure table in this validated
format.  The synthetic tables are test fixtures; the treatment result uses the
table produced by the dedicated transport run.

The pre-transport implementation is
\file{analysis\_transport/xsections/exposure\_folding.py}.  It validates this
table, loads the five fitted nominal curves and their 1000 replicas, applies
linear interpolation with the configured reaction thresholds, performs the
folding, aggregates channels by isotope, and calculates production budgets and
distal $R_{50}$.  Its Python step accumulator is a reference definition for
synthetic tests; the production scorer will perform the same accumulation
during the dedicated Geant4 run.  The detailed input and output schemas are
recorded in \file{data/xsections/SCHEMA.md}.

The required companion \file{exposure\_meta.json} carries the exposure-file
digest, run identifier, primary count, target-box dose, \file{Np\_per\_Gy},
Geant4 version, physics list, random seed, software revision, coordinate
definition and authoritative bin edges.  The normalization satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \file{Np\_per\_Gy}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{protons}}}
 {D_{\mathrm{sim,target}}},
 \label{eq:np-per-gy}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\mathrm{sim,target}}$ is the same \file{target\_dose\_Gy} written by
the exposure mode and consumed by \file{decay\_sampling/budget.py}.  Folded yields are
reported for the simulated run, per primary proton and per Gy.

The exposure-mode run directory additionally contains
\file{depth\_dose.csv}, \file{native\_route\_counts.csv}, the proposal-bank
data and their metadata.  The run tag includes the physics list.  Every product
records the proton count, dose convention, coordinate system, Geant4 version,
random seed and software revision.

\section{Direct folding of external cross sections}

Let $s$ be a proton step with length $\ell_s$, representative energy $E_s$,
and statistical weight $a_s$.  Let $n_{t,s}$ be the number density of target
isotope $t$ in the material traversed by that step.  The expected number of
residual nuclei produced through channel $c$ for replica $r$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta N_{s,c}^{(r)}=
  a_s n_{t(c),s}\ell_s\sigma_c^{(r)}(E_s).
  \label{eq:step-yield}
\end{equation}
The implementation converts millibarns to area before evaluating this
expression.  The production of isotope $j$ in spatial bin $m$ is
\begin{equation}
  P_{j,m}^{(r)}=
  \sum_{s\in m}\sum_{c\rightarrow j}
  a_s n_{t(c),s}\ell_s\sigma_c^{(r)}(E_s).
  \label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
The nominal calculation substitutes the pointwise replica median
$\sigma_c^{(\mathrm{nom})}(E)$.  Every replica substitutes one complete, correlated
curve.  This construction preserves correlations across energy and between
all spatial regions sampled by the same proton field.

For the five channels in Eq.~\eqref{eq:channels}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:step-yield}
and \eqref{eq:profile} define the complete production tally.  Residual nuclei
created for these channels by the Geant4 final-state model are omitted from
that tally.  Geant4 remains responsible for proton transport, secondary-proton
transport, continuous energy loss, multiple scattering, nuclear attenuation,
dose and positron transport.  This division assigns selected-channel
production to the experimentally fitted cross sections and avoids counting the
same production twice.

The method holds the proton fluence fixed while a selected partial production
cross section varies.  The relevant partial cross sections are tens of
millibarns, compared with a total proton inelastic cross section of several
hundred millibarns.  A tracked diagnostic will evaluate
\begin{equation}
  f(E)=\frac{\sum_c\sigma_c(E)}{\sigma_{\mathrm{inel}}^{(\mathrm{G4})}(E)}
  \label{eq:fluence-check}
\end{equation}
over the energies and materials sampled by the treatment field.  This ratio
sets the scale of the first-order attenuation correction associated with the
fixed-transport approximation.

\section{Production sites and positron transport}

Equation~\eqref{eq:profile} yields expected production rather than an integer
list of residual nuclei.  A common proposal sample converts the expectation
into a spatial source.  A step segment $s$ and channel $c$ are sampled from a
fixed proposal density $q_{s,c}$ that covers every selected target, energy and
position.  For replica $r$, its source contribution is proportional to
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta N_{s,c}^{(r)}}{q_{s,c}}.
  \label{eq:proposal}
\end{equation}
The nominal and all replicas use the same proposal sites and random-number
streams.  The resulting pairing reduces Monte Carlo noise in the displacement
between two edge estimates.

The variant-source mode creates the selected residual isotope at the sampled
production site and transports its positron through the local Geant4 material.
For detector compatibility, each variant writes the complete core
\file{emitters.csv} interface used by \file{stageA\_transport}: event and
isotope identifiers together with production and annihilation coordinates.
The companion metadata retain the exposure-run and proposal-bank digests,
cross-section variant identifier, fit digest, physics list, random seed and
normalization.

An extended event product records the production channel, target isotope,
proton energy at production, proposal probability and source weight.  These
fields support paired comparisons and audit the conversion from the proposal
bank to each variant source.  The legacy-compatible core file remains the
detector-facing interface; the extended product provides the additional
information required by the uncertainty analysis.

The existing beam-off handoff then applies isotope half-lives and acquisition
timing to the production budgets.  The nominal source proceeds through the
full detector simulation and reconstruction.  The replica ensemble is carried
by the weighted source bank defined in \file{docs/shared\_plan.tex}; variant
sources regenerated for selected replicas validate that weighted propagation.
Production-profile, annihilation-profile and reconstructed-edge displacements
remain distinct outputs throughout the analysis.

The longitudinal outputs distinguish the raw sum
\file{all\_production} from a timing-weighted activity profile.  A profile such
as \file{all\_inroom} is constructed from the named scenario in
\file{config/handoff\_scenarios.toml}.  The folding code and
\file{budget.py} use the same scenario resolver.  Output metadata record the
scenario-file digest, resolved irradiation, delay and measurement times, and
the isotope-specific handoff factors.

\section{Hadronic-transport envelope}

The transport calculation will accept a Geant4 reference physics list at run
time.  The first set is
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \file{QGSP\_BIC\_HP};
  \item \file{QGSP\_INCLXX\_HP};
  \item \file{QGSP\_BERT\_HP}.
\end{itemize}
Each physics-list name is recorded in the run metadata and run tag.  Each run
produces an independent target-resolved proton exposure table.  The same
nominal EXFOR curves are folded with the three tables.  The signed changes in
$b_{\mathrm{prod}}$ and $b_{\mathrm{reco}}$ are reported individually, and
the largest absolute change relative to BIC defines the initial envelope.
This envelope probes changes in primary and secondary proton transport.  The
external production cross sections remain common to all three calculations.

\section{Uncertainty estimator}

All 1000 cross-section replicas can be evaluated on one stored proton exposure
table without repeating proton transport.  The folding consists of array
operations over channel, energy and spatial bins.  The computationally
expensive positron and detector stages use a fixed proposal sample.  If the
downstream sample size makes propagation of all replicas impractical, a
stratified subset is selected across quantiles of the production-edge shift
and isotope-yield changes; the full ensemble still defines the production
uncertainty.

For either edge observable, let $q_{16}$, $q_{50}$ and $q_{84}$ be the
percentiles of the paired replica displacement.  The reported central bias is
$q_{50}$, the asymmetric interval is $(q_{50}-q_{16},q_{84}-q_{50})$, and the
single-number cross-section uncertainty is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{xs}}=\frac{q_{84}-q_{16}}{2}.
  \label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The full displacement distribution and isotope-resolved production changes are
retained with the scalar summary.

\section{Energy-grid convergence}

The scorer first writes a deliberately fine energy grid.  Candidate coarser
tables are constructed offline from the same transport realization.  For a
set $G$ of fine cells forming one coarse cell,
\begin{equation}
 K_G=\sum_{g\in G}K_g,
 \qquad
 \bar E_G=\frac{\sum_{g\in G}K_g\bar E_g}{K_G}.
 \label{eq:coarsen}
\end{equation}
Coarsening therefore preserves the exposure and its first energy moment.  The
tested approximation is the replacement of the exposure-weighted mean cross
section by $\sigma(\bar E_G)$ when the excitation function is nonlinear within
the coarse cell.

Every candidate table is folded with the nominal curve and all replicas.  The
nominal result and each replica are paired between the fine and coarse tables.
For each isotope, let $u_Y$ and $u_R$ be the fine-grid 16th--84th percentile
half-widths of the yield and production-edge displacement.  A candidate passes
when the largest absolute paired yield and edge changes are below $0.1u_Y$ and
$0.1u_R$, respectively, and its absolute edge change is below
\SI{0.1}{mm}.  The changes in the reported yield and edge half-widths must
satisfy the same limits.  A vanishing replica half-width admits only numerical
round-off from coarsening.

The moderate-statistics fine-grid transport determines the accepted binning.
The high-statistics run then uses that frozen grid.  The convergence table,
tolerances and pass decision are generated by tracked code and recorded in the
folding provenance.

\section{Production outside the folded channel set}

The folded source contains the five proton-induced channels in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:channels}.  Native Geant4 residuals provide a separate diagnostic
of routes outside this set, including neutron-induced production and the
$^{10}$C and $^{14}$O residuals.  The transport scorer records native counts by
projectile, target, residual isotope and depth.  These counts are classified
as represented or unrepresented by the folded set and passed through the same
named handoff scenarios.

The diagnostic reports both the raw-production fraction and the expected
beam-off measured fraction outside the folded set.  It is evaluated for BIC,
INCL++ and Bertini, and the largest fraction is retained as the conservative
first-pass estimate.  The folded source obtains its production sites, isotope
budgets and normalization from the direct estimator.  The native-counter input
digest and unmodeled fractions are copied into the folding provenance with a
diagnostic-only flag.

\section{Validation requirements}

The implementation is accepted when the following checks pass:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A uniform-slab calculation reproduces the analytic expectation
        $N=n\ell\sigma$ for a constant input cross section.
  \item Tabulated cross sections are reproduced at their grid points, remain
        zero at and below the configured thresholds, and follow the documented
        interpolation rule between points.
  \item Subdivision of proton steps and refinement of the energy and spatial
        exposure-table bins leave the production budgets and edge positions
        within the replica-relative and \SI{0.1}{mm} tolerances.
  \item A direct per-step sum agrees with the binned track-length calculation.
  \item Substituting the nominal and a selected replica reproduces their known
        production-ratio change in a mono-energetic slab.
  \item The selected-channel source contains only the externally folded tally,
        and a dedicated counter verifies the residual-nucleus exclusion.
  \item Dose and the stored proton track-length field are identical for every
        replica evaluated on a frozen transport.
  \item Fixed seeds reproduce the proposal sites, isotope budgets and paired
        edge shifts.
  \item With matched beam, phantom and physics-list settings, the new exposure
        application and \file{stageA\_transport} agree statistically in target
        dose and depth-dose shape.
  \item The variant-source mode reproduces the core \file{emitters.csv} schema
        and positron-range reference distributions while its extended event
        table preserves the channel, target, proton energy, proposal weight
        and variant provenance.
  \item Exposure metadata reproduce \file{Np\_per\_Gy} from the primary count
        and target-box dose, and folding and handoff resolve identical named-
        scenario factors.
  \item Native-route counters reproduce their raw and beam-off classified
        fractions without contributing to the folded source.
  \item Positron-range distributions from the externally sampled sources agree
        with the corresponding isotope and material reference calculations.
  \item Alternative physics lists write distinct run directories and record
        the selected list in their metadata.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implementation sequence}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Define the tracked schema for the binned proton exposure table and
        implement cross-section interpolation, Eq.~\eqref{eq:step-yield},
        channel aggregation and $R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}$ using synthetic tables.
  \item Validate the folding code with analytic slab, units, threshold,
        interpolation, per-step--versus--binned and bin-refinement tests.
  \item Implement run-metadata validation, per-Gy normalization, named-scenario
        activity profiles, vectorized outputs, convergence analysis and the
        native-route diagnostic.
  \item Create the independent \file{stageA\_xsection\_ensemble/} Geant4
        application and implement its proton-exposure mode, metadata, dose,
        native-route counters and proposal bank.  Validate it with analytic
        slabs and matched depth-dose comparisons to \file{stageA\_transport}.
  \item Generate a moderate-statistics nominal SOBP exposure run.  This run
        constructs the physical target--energy--position exposure table and
        freezes the proposal bank used by all cross-section variants.
  \item Fold the nominal curves and all 1000 replicas, then write isotope
        budgets, production profiles, $R_{50,\mathrm{prod}}$ values and the
        paired uncertainty distribution.
  \item Implement the variant-source mode.  Write the detector-compatible
        \file{emitters.csv}, extended event information and complete variant
        provenance while preserving both production and annihilation
        coordinates.
  \item Apply the existing beam-off handoff and propagate the nominal source
        and a stratified replica subset through the downstream detector and
        reconstruction calculation.
  \item Repeat the proton transport with INCL++ and Bertini, fold the common
        external curves and report the independent transport envelope.
  \item Generate the final high-statistics source after the closure tests and
        pilot convergence criteria have been satisfied.
\end{enumerate}

All calculations, tables and figures are produced by tracked repository code.
The generated data record the input fit version, transport run identifier,
physics list, Geant4 version, random seed, binning and software revision.
